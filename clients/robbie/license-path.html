Step one before anything else

Look her up. It's free and it takes two minutes.

Ohio's license database is public, needs no account, and updates daily. You do not have to ask Robbie anything uncomfortable — just look.

  1. Go to elicense.ohio.gov and choose License Look-Up

    Desktop Chrome or Firefox works best. elicense.ohio.gov/oh_verifylicense

  2. Set the board to "Cosmetology and Barber Board"

    Search by last name and first initial if you don't have a license number.

  3. Open the matching record → View More Info → Printer Friendly Version

    Save it as a PDF. Sola will want proof of licensure, and this is it — free, official, instant.

What you're looking for

The license type and the status. Type tells you what she can legally charge for. Status tells you whether it's active or lapsed — a lapsed license is a much smaller problem than no license, and is usually fixed with a restoration fee rather than school.

Common, and not a character judgment — loc work sat in a regulatory grey zone for years and plenty of excellent locticians never went through the state. But a suite complex will ask for the license before she signs, so this becomes the gate.

The find worth chasing

Ohio's rules for out-of-state applicants let a year of licensed experience substitute for 100 hours of training, up to 500 hours. Robbie's six years would cap out at 500 — more than the 450 the Natural Hair Stylist license requires.

The catch is in one word: licensed. The provision sits in the out-of-state license holder rule, so it most likely applies only if she has held a license somewhere. Six years of unlicensed Ohio practice probably doesn't qualify. But it costs one email to find out, and if it applies it saves $7,500 and a season of her life. It's question 7 in the draft below.

The stack three credentials, not one

What a suite renter actually needs in Ohio

Almost everyone knows about the first one. The second and third are where suite renters get caught.

  1. Her practicing license

    Natural Hair Stylist or Cosmetologist. This is what the lookup shows. It defines her menu.

  2. An Independent Contractor license

    Ohio requires this separately for someone working as an independent inside a licensed salon. Around $75 to issue, $70 to renew. This is the one suite renters forget, and it is exactly Robbie's situation.

  3. A salon license for the suite address

    Ohio licenses salons by location. Whether Sola holds one for the whole building or each suite needs its own is the single question nobody online can answer. Ask Sola in writing and confirm with the Board.

The sharp edge — worth asking about specifically

Ohio Revised Code §4713.41 describes a salon license being issued on the affirmation that an individual holding a current, valid cosmetologist license has charge of and immediate supervision over the salon whenever it's open.

Read literally, that raises a real question: if Robbie holds only a Natural Hair Stylist license, can she be the licence-holder and supervisor of her own suite? There may well be a parallel provision for natural hair and boutique salons — but if there isn't, this quietly determines whether she can hold her own salon license or must operate under Sola's. It's question 6 below, and it's the one I'd want answered before signing anything.

Branch C only if she needs the 450 hours

Ohio's only accredited natural hair program is in Columbus

A genuine piece of luck. She wouldn't have to leave the city or settle for a general cosmetology program that treats loc work as an afterthought.

The 450-hour route

New Directions Beauty Institute

2013 E. Dublin-Granville Rd, Columbus, OH 43229. Ohio's only accredited natural hair program, open since 2010. Curriculum covers state law, salon operations, and salon ownership alongside technique — useful for someone about to run her own suite.

$7,500 · 13 weeks full-time or 22.5 weeks part-time. The part-time track is the one that lets her keep her book while she does it.

The narrow route

Boutique Service Registration

A shorter registration route covering braiding specifically, requiring a notarized affidavit from a trainer. Columbus Braiding Co runs a course for it.

Worth knowing, probably not the answer. If it covers braiding but not locking, it doesn't cover her core service. Confirm the scope before spending a dollar on it — that's question 1.

Order of operations

Ask before you enroll

Send the email first. If the experience-credit provision applies to her, she may need far fewer than 450 hours — or none. Paying $7,500 before asking a free question would be the most expensive mistake in this whole plan.

Meanwhile

The suite doesn't have to wait for everything

The temporary pre-examination work permit exists precisely for this gap. Ask whether it lets her work in a suite while her exam is pending — that could compress the timeline by months.

The email send it this week

Subject: Licensing requirements - loc services in a rented salon suite (Columbus)

Good morning,

I am a natural hair stylist in Columbus with six years of experience
specializing in locs - installation, retwisting, interlocking, and
maintenance. I am preparing to rent an individual studio in a multi-suite
salon facility and want to make certain I am fully compliant before I
sign anything.

My current status: [state it plainly - e.g. "I hold Natural Hair Stylist
license #______" or "I do not currently hold an Ohio license"].

I would be grateful for written answers to the following:

1. SCOPE. Which license permits loc installation, retwisting,
   interlocking, and maintenance - Natural Hair Stylist, Cosmetologist,
   or a Boutique Service Registration? Does a Boutique Service
   Registration in braiding cover locking, or braiding only?

2. CUTTING. Does the Natural Hair Stylist license permit trimming or
   shaping locs?

3. CHEMICAL SERVICES. I understand the Natural Hair Stylist license is
   non-chemical. Please confirm whether coloring locs requires a
   Cosmetologist license.

4. INDEPENDENT CONTRACTOR LICENSE. Is an Independent Contractor license
   required to rent a studio in a multi-suite facility? What is the
   application process, fee, and processing time?

5. SALON LICENSE. In a multi-suite facility where each stylist rents an
   individual studio, does the facility operator hold a single salon
   license for the building, or must each individual studio be
   separately licensed as a salon? If each studio requires its own
   license, please describe the requirements, fee, and inspection
   process.

6. SUPERVISION. ORC 4713.41 refers to an individual holding a valid
   Cosmetologist license having charge of and immediate supervision over
   a salon. If I hold a Natural Hair Stylist license rather than a
   Cosmetologist license, may I be the license holder and supervisor of
   my own studio, or is a Cosmetologist required in that role?

7. EXPERIENCE CREDIT. I understand Ohio may substitute 100 hours of
   training for each year of licensed experience, up to 500 hours. Does
   that provision apply only to applicants licensed in another state, or
   can documented Ohio work experience be credited toward the 450-hour
   Natural Hair Stylist requirement?

8. WORK PERMIT. Does the temporary pre-examination work permit allow me
   to work in a rented studio while my examination is pending?

9. CONTINUING EDUCATION. What CE is required for my license type, and
   what is the renewal cycle?

10. POSTING. What must be displayed in my studio - licenses,
    certificates, price list, or anything else an inspector will look
    for?

Thank you very much for your time. A written reply would be a great
help, as I expect to be asked for documentation by the facility and by
my insurer.

Kind regards,
[Full name]
[Phone] | [Email]
[License number, if any]

While you wait for the reply

  • Run the eLicense lookup and save the PDF. Two minutes, and it settles which branch she's on.
  • Ask Sola the salon-license question in writing too. Their answer and the Board's should match. If they don't, that's worth knowing before signing.
  • Don't enroll in anything yet. Question 7 could make the $7,500 unnecessary.
  • Everything else in the suite plan continues. The LLC, the EIN, the business bank account, the bookkeeping, the client list — none of it waits on licensing.
